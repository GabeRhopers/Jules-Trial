# Claims Table (Stage 4)

Claims about what the book says are secondhand (access level C). Verdicts are about the evidence behind each claim.
Fields searched: cognitive psychology, behavioral economics, sleep science, organizational behavior.

| ID | Claim (paraphrased) | Location | Verdict | Best evidence | Evidence level | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-01 | Success leads to a "clarity paradox" that undermines future success | Ch. 1 | Unverifiable | The author's own model, introduced in his 2012 HBR essay; no study tests it | Expert opinion | [HBR 2012](https://hbr.org/2012/08/the-disciplined-pursuit-of-less) |
| C-02 | Juran's "vital few": a small share of problems drives most quality loss | Ch. 3 | Verified (attribution) | Juran applied Pareto's observation to quality and popularized "vital few, trivial many" (later "useful many"). Dates in secondary sources vary (1941 vs. 1951). The 80/20 split is a rule of thumb, not a law | Historical, secondary | [Wikipedia: Joseph Juran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_M._Juran) · [iSixSigma](https://www.isixsigma.com/dictionary/trivial-many/) |
| C-03 | Learned helplessness explains how we forget we can choose | Ch. 2 | Partially supported | The original researchers revised the theory in 2016: passivity is the default response to prolonged bad events, and what is learned is control. The book's practical point (noticing you have control) fits the revised view, but the mechanism it describes is the outdated one | Synthesis of animal lab research | [Maier & Seligman 2016, Psychological Review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4920136/) |
| C-04 | Best violinists slept 8.6 h a day and napped 2.8 h a week | Ch. 8 | Verified | Exact figures appear in the original study. Caveats: 30 students, one diary week, correlational; the comparison group was music teachers (7.8 h) | Observational | [Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Romer 1993, Psychological Review 100](https://graphics8.nytimes.com/images/blogs/freakonomics/pdf/DeliberatePractice%28PsychologicalReview%29.pdf) |
| C-05 | Deliberate practice is the main thing separating the best violinists | Ch. 8 | Contested | A preregistered replication (2019) did not reproduce the core finding; practice mattered, but much less than the original reported | Preregistered replication | [Macnamara & Maitra 2019, Royal Society Open Science](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6731745/) |
| C-06 | Too little sleep damages your thinking | Ch. 8 | Verified | Meta-analysis of 70 articles: short-term sleep deprivation clearly hurts attention and working memory; effect on reasoning accuracy was small and not significant | Meta-analysis | [Lim & Dinges 2010, Psychological Bulletin](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20438143/) |
| C-07 | Play broadens options, relieves stress and boosts executive function | Ch. 7 | Partially supported | Adult play research is thin and mostly correlational (play linked to lower stress and better functioning). No adult study found for the executive-function claim | Observational | [Van Vleet & Feeney 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26386001/) · [2023 integrative review](https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2023.2288955) |
| C-08 | The 90 Percent Rule improves decisions | Ch. 9 | Unverifiable | A heuristic; no study found | Expert opinion | Source Brief |
| C-09 | Teams without a clear purpose drift into politics or scattered effort | Ch. 10 | Unverifiable | The author's consulting observation ("In my work, I have noticed...") | Expert opinion, anecdote | [Reader notes](https://www.grahammann.net/book-notes/essentialism-the-disciplined-pursuit-of-less-greg-mckeown) |
| C-10 | Sunk-cost bias keeps us in losing commitments | Ch. 12 | Verified | Meta-analysis of 98 effect sizes (1976 to 2013) confirms the effect; it is stronger in younger people, and economic training did not reduce it | Meta-analysis | [Roth, Robbert & Straus 2015, Business Research](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40685-014-0014-8) |
| C-11 | The endowment effect makes us overvalue what we own | Ch. 12 | Contested | Plott & Zeiler (2005) argued the gap depends on experimental procedures; Fehr, Hakimov & Kubler (2015) found it persists. Still debated | Experiments, mixed replications | [Plott & Zeiler debate (AER 2011)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:aea:aecrev:v:101:y:2011:i:2:p:1012-28) · [Fehr et al. 2015](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:eecrev:v:78:y:2015:i:c:p:120-128) |
| C-12 | We underestimate how long tasks take (planning fallacy) | Ch. 15 | Verified | Term from Kahneman and Tversky (1979; the book credits Kahneman alone). Students predicted thesis completion in 33.9 days on average and took 55.5 | Experiments, widely replicated | [Buehler, Griffin & Ross 1994, JPSP 67](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/buehler-et-al-1994.pdf) |
| C-13 | Progress, even small wins, is the strongest motivator | Ch. 17 | Partially supported | Diary study: 238 people, 26 project teams, 7 companies, nearly 12,000 entries. Strong but observational, one research program, and about workday motivation, broader than "all forms of human motivation" | Observational | [HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/how-small-wins-unleash-creativity) |
| C-14 | Routine makes the essential automatic | Ch. 18 | Verified | Repeating a behavior in a stable context builds automaticity; median 66 days, range 18 to 254, so it takes longer than popular "21 days" claims | Field study | [Lally et al. 2010, Eur. J. Social Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674) |
